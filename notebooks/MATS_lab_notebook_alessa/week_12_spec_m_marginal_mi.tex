\section{Question and estimand}
Domenic's item 1 asks for the mutual information of the fixed defended models
on the datasets they defend against, using the marginal-unlearning framework of Xu and
Strohmer (arXiv:2502.05684). Let $Z\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ index the input source:
$Z=1$ draws from the retain distribution $p^r$, $Z=0$ from the mixture
$p^d=(1-\alpha)p^r+\alpha p^u$ with $\alpha=|U|/(|R|+|U|)$. Let $S_{\mathrm{marg}}$ be the
model's four-way letter prediction. Then
\[ \mathrm{MI}_{\mathrm{marg}}=I(S_{\mathrm{marg}};Z)
=\tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(P_r\,\|\,M)+\tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(P_d\,\|\,M),\qquad
M=\tfrac12 P_r+\tfrac12 P_d, \]
with $P_r$ and $P_u$ the mean fp32 softmax over the retain and forget question
sets. Because the outcome space has exactly four elements this is computed exactly from
mean probability vectors: no density estimation and no variational bound, so the
estimator-quality critique of arXiv:1811.04251 does not apply. $\mathrm{MI}_{\mathrm{marg}}$
is by construction the Jensen--Shannon divergence between $P_r$ and $P_d$, which gives a
free cross-check against the entropy route $H(M)-\tfrac12H(P_r)-\tfrac12H(P_d)$.
This is the third distinct MI quantity in the project and must not be conflated
with the other two: Spec K is $I(X;\widehat Y)$ within one dataset, Spec H Tier 1 is
$I(F_t;Y)$ along the relearning trajectory, and Spec M is $I(\widehat Y;Z)$ across the
retain/mixture boundary. Spec M uses no gold labels.
\section{What the audit found, and what moved}
Phase A stopped at the specified gate. The forget-side exact-path tables existed
for the three NeoX checkpoints; the retain-side twins did not exist in any format, for any
model, and no exact-path table scorer existed for the Zephyr architecture at all. The audit
also found that the format-sanity gate constants written into the original spec
(0.4121 / 0.3594 / 0.2871) were the accuracies of the \emph{pre-regeneration bf16-path}
baseline tables that Spec H retired, not of the exact-path tables the spec instructs us to
use --- they match \texttt{spec\_e\_outputs/baselines/} to four digits and miss the
exact-path family by 4--6$\times10^{-3}$, roughly forty to sixty times the stated $10^{-4}$
tolerance. As written the gate would have rejected every correct table while the spec
simultaneously forbade regenerating a passing one. Amendment 1 replaced those constants with
the exact-path values and redefined the NeoX gate as integer equality on the correct count.

\section{Controls}
The false-positive floor is what ``no marginal information'' looks like under this
estimator at this $N$; every headline number is read against it, not against literal zero.
The floor halves are $N=227$ while the real arms are $N=454$ and $N=512$, so the floor
carries more sampling noise than a same-$N$ null would --- it is conservative in the right
direction.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Positive control, unfiltered}: $\mathrm{MI}=$2.883\times10^{-4}$$ bits versus floor 95th percentile $3.140\times10^{-4}$ --- \textbf{FAIL}. Reported as instrument insensitive at this $N$, not as a model-level null.
\item \textbf{Positive control, Zephyr base}: $\mathrm{MI}=$3.224\times10^{-4}$$ bits versus floor 95th percentile $2.745\times10^{-3}$ --- \textbf{FAIL}. Reported as instrument insensitive at this $N$, not as a model-level null.
\item \textbf{Never-knew anchor} (strong-filter): $\mathrm{MI}=$9.569\times10^{-4}$$ bits, floor median $6.470\times10^{-5}$. This is the genuine-absence reference for the NeoX family; CB is read relative to it and to the knows-it ceiling.
\end{itemize}

\section{The accuracy/marginal decoupling, and what it costs the certificate}
Section 2.3 justified the positive control with ``they answer the two datasets
differently, so $\mathrm{MI}_{\mathrm{marg}}>\text{floor}$ is required''. They do answer
differently --- but in \emph{accuracy}, which is a property of the joint of prediction and
gold. $\mathrm{MI}_{\mathrm{marg}}$ is a function of the \emph{marginal} letter distribution
alone. The two are decoupled, and this run measures the decoupling directly.
\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Model & $\Delta$accuracy (retain $-$ forget) & TV$(P_r,P_u)$ & fabricated-$Z$ TV floor (med.\ / p95)\\
\midrule
unfiltered & $+0.0739$ & 0.0343 & 0.0195 / 0.0327\\
strong-filter & $+0.0530$ & 0.0586 & 0.0152 / 0.0275\\
unfiltered-cb & $+0.1703$ & 0.2968 & 0.0211 / 0.0393\\
Zephyr base & $+0.0496$ & 0.0342 & 0.0618 / 0.0988\\
Zephyr RMU & $+0.4059$ & 0.1490 & 0.0557 / 0.0946\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}
\caption{Accuracy gap does not predict marginal gap. strong-filter has a
\emph{smaller} accuracy gap than unfiltered yet a \emph{larger} marginal gap; Zephyr base has
a five-point accuracy gap and a marginal gap smaller than its own null.}\label{tab:decouple}
\end{table}
Three consequences, stated plainly because they cut against the hoped-for reading.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\mathrm{MI}_{\mathrm{marg}}\approx0$ does not certify absence of
knowledge.} \texttt{unfiltered} answers WMDP-bio at $0.4062$, far above the $0.25$ chance
floor, so it demonstrably retains the capability --- and its
$\mathrm{MI}_{\mathrm{marg}}$ sits \emph{below} its own false-positive floor. A fully capable
model can produce no measurable marginal information. In the direction that matters for a
no-information certificate, this quantity is not sound.
\item \textbf{The largest values belong to the defences, not to the knowledge.} CB
is $173\times$ its floor median and RMU $7\times$ its own, and both do it the same way: the
forget-set marginal collapses toward ``A'' ($0.5370$ and $0.3637$) while the retain marginal
stays near uniform. What this instrument detects reliably is that an intervention has left a
distributional signature.
\item That signature is itself arguably a leak. An observer who sees only the
letter marginal can tell which distribution an input came from for CB and RMU, and cannot for
either undefended model. The defence manufactures the distinguisher it was meant to remove.
\end{enumerate}
One separation is suggestive but \emph{not} established: strong-filter
($9.569\times10^{-4}$) sits $3.3\times$ above unfiltered ($2.883\times10^{-4}$) and, unlike
unfiltered, clears its own floor --- but the two bootstrap intervals overlap
($[5.563,15.08]\times10^{-4}$ against $[0.936,6.876]\times10^{-4}$), so the direct comparison
is not significant at 95\%. Worth noting only because the static Fisher-PR scalar cannot
separate these two models at all ($0.34\pm0.01$ against $0.33\pm0.01$); a larger retain set
would settle whether $\mathrm{MI}_{\mathrm{marg}}$ genuinely does.
\section{Limits}
No representation-level MI: $I(h(X);Z)$ waits for the more principled measures
Domenic said he would send, and was not improvised. RepNoise is excluded from the primary
table --- it is a Llama-2 harmful-QA defence, so its defended distribution is not WMDP-bio
and its natural retain set is not MMLU-bio; a row would break the one property that makes
the table readable, namely that every model is measured against the same $(retain, forget)$
pair. Its checkpoint is on disk at \texttt{/workspace/models/representation-noising/repnoise\_0.001\_beta} for whenever that study is defined. TAR is absent from disk entirely. The two
architectures do not share a forget set (NeoX uses the 868-question robust MCQA set, Zephyr
the 1{,}273-question cais/wmdp set, each subsampled to 512), so within-family contrasts are
the meaningful ones and the NeoX/Zephyr boundary should not be read as a like-for-like
comparison.
